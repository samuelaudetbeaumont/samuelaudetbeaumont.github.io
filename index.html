Samuel Audet-Beaumont

Ph.D. Candidate in Pure Mathematics · Université Laval · Québec, Canada

I am a Ph.D. candidate in pure mathematics at Université Laval, supervised by Alexandre Girouard. My research lies in spectral geometry, with particular interests in Steklov eigenvalue problems, high multiplicity constructions, spectral discretization, and representation-theoretic methods.

Broadly, I study the interplay between continuous and discrete spectral problems: on one hand, using graph-based and symmetry-based ideas to construct manifolds with remarkable spectral behaviour; on the other, refining discretization schemes that approximate spectral data from finite geometric information.

Research

My work focuses on spectral geometry and graph-based methods. One direction concerns constructing manifolds with large eigenvalue multiplicities, especially through symmetry and representation theory. Another concerns the analysis and refinement of discretization schemes for Steklov, Neumann, and related spectral problems.

I am interested in how discrete models can both illuminate geometric spectral phenomena and provide rigorous approximations of spectra arising from smooth spaces.
