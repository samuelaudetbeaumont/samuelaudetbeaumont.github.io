Samuel Audet-Beaumont

Ph.D. Candidate in Pure Mathematics · Université Laval · Québec, Canada

I am a Ph.D. candidate in pure mathematics at Université Laval, supervised by Alexandre Girouard. My research is in spectral geometry, with an emphasis on Steklov eigenvalue problems, graph discretization, eigenvalue multiplicity, and applications of representation theory.

Broadly, I study how geometric and spectral information can be transferred between manifolds and finite weighted graphs, both to construct examples with interesting spectral properties and to approximate spectra from discrete data.

Research

My work combines spectral geometry, analysis, and graph-based methods. One direction concerns constructing surfaces with large eigenvalue multiplicities, using symmetry and representation theory. Another concerns quantitative approximation of the Steklov and Neumann spectra by weighted graphs.

My current research interests also include spectral crossings between different representation types, equivariant Steklov homogenization, and spectral discretization using ambient distances and sampled data.

Teaching

I have taught Mathematics for Engineering III at Université Laval (Fall 2023 and Fall 2024), and have worked as a teaching assistant and tutor for undergraduate mathematics courses since 2021. I have also mentored students through research projects and directed readings.

My approach emphasizes geometric intuition, student participation, and adapting explanations to students' needs. I received the Faculty of Science and Engineering's Star Teacher distinction in 2023 and 2024.
